# The Perfect Walk

The Perfect Walk turns an ordinary morning walk into a five-part practice.

The app is not meant to become another thing someone has to operate. Its job is to help someone enter the experience: a short cue creates an anchor point, then music, movement, attention and feeling take over. The screen should gradually become irrelevant.

The five parts provide different entrances into the practice, while the middle three can move with the user's own flow. The app doesn't prescribe what someone should feel. It creates space for them to discover their own experience.

The design principle I kept returning to was:

"If the phone disappeared into my pocket five minutes in and I forgot it was there, would the experience still work?"

That became the standard for the interface, audio behaviour and guidance.

The design decision I'm proudest of is progressive independence. Rather than giving everyone the same amount of guidance forever, the app gradually reduces its prompts as the practice becomes familiar: Discover, Remember, Trust, then Embody. Early walks provide more context and stronger anchors. Later walks get quieter. If someone needs support again, they can ask for it.

The goal isn't to make the app more engaging.

The goal is to make the practice more natural.
